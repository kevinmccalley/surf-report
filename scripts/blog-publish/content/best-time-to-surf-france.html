Europe's Premier Surf Destination

France's Basque and Landes coastline is the centre of European competitive surfing and one of the most visited surf destinations in the world. The combination of powerful, long-period Atlantic swell, straight sandy beaches that produce hollow beach break barrels, and a world-class food and wine culture has made the stretch between Lacanau and Biarritz — with Hossegor at its heart — an annual pilgrimage for surfers from across the continent and beyond.

The surf here is raw and powerful. Unlike Morocco's forgiving point breaks or California's consistent but mellow beach breaks, the beach breaks of the Landes are genuine high-performance waves — heavy-lipped barrels that reward precision surfing and punish mistakes. They have produced more World Surf League champions per square kilometre than almost anywhere else on earth.

The Swell Window

France's Atlantic coast faces northwest, directly into the teeth of the North Atlantic storm track. Between September and April, low-pressure systems moving across the North Atlantic generate northwest and west swells that arrive with enormous power on the beaches of the Landes. The shallow, straight sandy bottom creates the hollow, fast-breaking waves that characterise Hossegor's famous beach breaks.

The optimal window is September through November — the autumn season when swells are powerful and consistent, water temperature is still tolerable, and the summer crowds of European beachgoers have dissipated. This is also when the WSL Championship Tour holds its annual contest at La Gravière.

Month by Month

July – August (Summer)

Summer in France brings warm weather, warm water (around 22°C / 72°F), and enormous crowds — but small surf. The North Atlantic storm track retreats northward and swells are typically short-period, small, and irregular. The beach is beautiful; the surf is not at its best. Summer is for swimming and culture, not barrel hunting.

September (The Transition — Often the Best Month)

September is magical on the French Atlantic coast. The summer crowds vanish overnight with the return of school, the swell begins building, and the water temperature is still warm enough for a light wetsuit or even a spring suit on warm days. September swells are often the most perfectly sized of the year — consistent 1.5–2.5 m surf on the longer-period Atlantic swells that are beginning to rebuild. Lacanau and Biarritz are at their most enjoyable. Many experienced travellers consider September the single best month of the year to visit.

October (Peak Season)

October is when the French surf season peaks. The WSL Championship Tour event at Hossegor — now branded as the Quiksilver Pro — takes place in October, drawing the best surfers in the world to compete at La Gravière. The swell is consistent and powerful, often in the 2–3 m range with periods of 14–16 seconds. The beach break produces its most fearsome hollow barrels. Water temperature has dropped to around 17–18°C (63–64°F) — a 3/2 wetsuit is standard.

November (Power Swells)

November brings the most powerful swells of the year — large, heavy North Atlantic groundswells that can exceed 3 metres face height at La Gravière and send the lineup into near-survival mode. This is serious surfing territory. But November also brings exceptional quality for the periods between big swells: the water is clear, the crowds are thin, and the smaller swells that precede and follow each storm produce some of the cleanest, most perfectly shaped beach break barrels in Europe. Water temperature around 15–16°C (59–61°F) — a 4/3 is recommended.

December – February (Winter)

Winter in France is raw. Cold water (around 12–14°C / 54–57°F), strong winds, and north-to-northwest storms dominate. The surf can be massive and excellent during brief windows, but conditions are often challenging — strong onshore winds, heavy rain, and limited daylight hours. Only committed surfers with solid wetsuit setups (5/4, booties, gloves, hood) will enjoy it. For most visitors this is off-season.

March – April (Transition to Summer)

Spring brings warming water and improving conditions. Swells remain consistent from the still-active North Atlantic storm track. Crowds are minimal. This is an underrated time to visit — good surf, no queues, and the infrastructure of the surf industry is fully operational again after winter.

The Spots

La Gravière — The most powerful and prestigious beach break in Europe. Located on the northern edge of Hossegor, La Gravière produces deep, hollow barrels in both directions over a sand bottom that shifts constantly with the seasons. At its best on a northwest swell of 1.5–3 m with 14+ seconds period and a light offshore morning wind. When it is firing, it rivals Pipeline for sheer performance and intensity. This is not a beginner wave.

Les Culs Nuls — The beach break immediately south of La Gravière, typically slightly more sheltered and slightly more forgiving. Often a better option than La Gravière when the main break is too big or too crowded.

Biarritz — The Grande Plage at Biarritz is the most iconic surf beach in France — the place where surfing was introduced to Europe in 1957 when Peter Viertel rode waves in front of the Grand Casino. Today it is a broad beach break that works across a wide range of swell sizes and is accessible to all skill levels. The city itself is one of Europe's most beautiful, with Belle Époque architecture, outstanding Basque cuisine, and a surf culture that permeates every corner.

Anglet — Located between Biarritz and the mouth of the Adour River, Anglet is a long series of beach breaks separated by jetties. More consistent than Biarritz on smaller swells and less crowded than Hossegor. A good all-round option across the season.

Lacanau — A surf town 50 kilometres north of Bordeaux and one of France's most famous beach breaks. More exposed than Hossegor and picks up swell on a wider range of directions. Hosts a prestigious WSL Challenger Series event each summer. Good for all skill levels but at its best on moderate northwest swells.

Over the Border: Mundaka

No guide to surfing France would be complete without mentioning Mundaka, just across the border in Spain's Basque Country. Mundaka is a left-hand river mouth break — fed by the Urdaibai Biosphere Reserve — that produces one of the longest and most perfectly formed left barrels in Europe when the conditions align: north to northwest swell, 12+ seconds period, and low tide. It does not work every day, but when it does, it is one of the great waves of the world. The drive from Biarritz is under an hour.

Gear for the Landes

These are fast, hollow beach breaks with a sharp, sucking takeoff, not mellow point-break walls — pack accordingly. A step-down shortboard with a bit of extra foam and a wider tail handles the late, steep drops better than an ultra-thin performance board, and a slightly stiffer fin setup helps hold a rail in the powerful, shifting sandbanks. Reef boots aren't needed — it's all sand — but a leash rated for heavier surf is worth it given how much water moves through a set at La Gravière on a solid day.

Wetsuit-wise, a 3/2 covers September and October, a 4/3 handles November through into winter, and by December–February most surfers are in a 5/4 with boots, gloves, and a hood. Bring more than one suit thickness if your trip spans the shoulder seasons — the water temperature swing from September to December is significant.

When to Go

September is the recommendation for first-time visitors to the French Atlantic coast. The conditions are excellent, the crowds have gone, the water is warm enough for a light wetsuit, and the culture of the Basque region is at its most enjoyable.

October is the recommendation for experienced surfers who want to see France at its competitive best — powerful, consistent, and performing at the level that has produced world champions.

Check live forecasts at La Gravière, Biarritz, and Lacanau before planning any session.
