The Beach Break

A beach break is any wave that breaks over a sand bottom. Unlike a reef or point break — where the wave shape is largely determined by permanent, fixed underwater topography — a beach break is in constant flux. Sand moves with every tide, every swell, every storm. The sandbar that produced a perfect peak last week may be gone this week. This impermanence is both the frustration and the appeal: beach breaks are unpredictable, democratic, and capable of producing waves that rival anything in the world when the sand happens to be right.

The eleven waves on this list are the best of them.

1. Playa Zicatela (Puerto Escondido) — Oaxaca, Mexico

Puerto Escondido's main beach — Playa Zicatela — is known as the Mexican Pipeline, and the comparison is not hyperbole. The wave breaks directly on the beach with no reef, no channel, and no gradual build — south and southwest groundswells arrive from the deep Pacific and stand up almost instantaneously on the steep sand bottom, producing one of the most powerful and hollow shore-break environments in the world.

A Puerto Escondido barrel is not a subtle experience. The waves break fast, the sand bottom is unforgiving, and the hold-downs after a wipeout are long. The shallow inside section is genuinely dangerous in larger surf. It is also, when the swell is right and a surfer finds the barrel, one of the most spectacular waves on earth. The WSL has held events here. The wider town of Puerto Escondido has developed into a full surf destination, with a range of accommodation, excellent seafood, and an international community of wave-focused travellers.

Best season: May through October on south and southwest groundswells. Peak: June through September. Long swell period (14+ seconds) is key — short-period windswell produces powerful but disorganised surf.

2. La Gravière — Hossegor, France

Hossegor is the most consistent surf beach in Europe, and La Gravière is its centrepiece. The Landes coast of southwest France receives the full force of North Atlantic groundswells generated by winter low-pressure systems, and the sandbanks at Hossegor — shaped by the converging forces of the Atlantic and the nearby estuary — produce a hollow, powerful beach break that has hosted WSL Championship Tour events for decades.

La Gravière at its best is a genuine world-class left and right beach break, throwing thick lips over a sand bottom that can be treacherously shallow on the inside. The wave holds size well and at 1.5–2.5m (5–8ft) is as good as anything in Europe. The surrounding culture — the Basque-influenced cuisine, the pine forests of the Landes, the relaxed but serious surf community — makes Hossegor one of the great surf trips on the continent.

Best season: September through November for the combination of consistent Atlantic swells, warmer water (18–20°C / 64–68°F), and manageable crowds. The prime Atlantic swell window runs October through March, but winter water temperature drops to 10–14°C (50–57°F) requiring a 5/4 wetsuit and often hoods and gloves.

3. Supertubos — Peniche, Portugal

Supertubos is Portugal's answer to Puerto Escondido — a powerful, hollow beach break on the Peniche peninsula that stands up on a steep sand bottom and throws heavy, fast barrels in both directions. The WSL has held Peniche events here for years, and the wave is consistently rated among the best beach breaks in Europe. Unlike La Gravière, which can be unpredictable depending on sandbar quality, Supertubos tends to produce consistent quality across a wider range of swell sizes and directions.

The Peniche peninsula's geography — a narrow spit of land jutting into the Atlantic — means there is almost always a side that has rideable conditions regardless of wind direction. The town of Peniche is a working fishing port rather than a resort, which keeps the character authentic and the food excellent.

Best season: October through March on northwest Atlantic groundswells. Water temperature in winter: 14–16°C (57–61°F) — a 4/3 or 5/4 wetsuit depending on the month. Summer brings crowds and smaller, less consistent surf.

4. Playa Hermosa — Central Pacific, Costa Rica

Playa Hermosa (south of Jacó, distinct from several other beaches sharing the same name in Costa Rica) is one of the most consistent and powerful beach breaks in Central America. The dark sand beach receives south and southwest swells cleanly, and the powerful, hollow peaks that form here have made it a WSL competition venue and a year-round destination for surfers looking for above-average beach break quality in a warm, accessible location.

The waves can be very powerful — Playa Hermosa draws more comparison to Puerto Escondido than to the mellow beginner breaks further north in Guanacaste — and the conditions suit intermediate to advanced surfers on medium to larger swells. The surrounding area is a working surf destination rather than a resort: reasonable accommodation, solid food, and a community of surfers who live there year-round.

Best season: May through November on south and southwest swells. Water temperature: 26–28°C (79–82°F) year-round — boardshorts.

5. Huntington Beach — Orange County, California

Surf City USA. Huntington Beach is the most iconic beach break in North America — a long, consistent stretch of sand centred around the pier that has hosted the US Open of Surfing for decades and given the California beach break its cultural identity. The pier creates a sand-funnelling effect that consistently produces quality peaks on both sides, and the combination of summer south swells and autumn northwest groundswells keeps the wave working for much of the year.

Huntington is not the most powerful or hollow beach break in the world. What it offers is consistency, accessibility, and the deep cultural richness of a break that has been at the centre of California surfing for generations. The US Open, held each summer, transforms the area around the pier into the largest free surf competition in the world.

Best season: June through October for south swells in summer; October through December for the early northwest swells of the season. Best in the morning before the sea breeze fills in.

6. Fistral Beach — Newquay, Cornwall, UK

Fistral is the most famous surf beach in the United Kingdom — a west-facing bay on the north Cornish coast that receives Atlantic swells with open-ocean directness and produces consistent, quality beach break waves across a wide range of conditions. It is the home beach of the British surf industry, hosts multiple major competitions annually, and has been central to the growth of surfing in the UK since the 1960s.

The wave is not typically as powerful as Hossegor or Supertubos, but it is consistent, well-shaped, and benefits from good bathymetry that organises the peaks across the bay. The surrounding Newquay area has developed a genuine surf culture — board shapers, surf schools, and a year-round community of local surfers who treat the Cornish Atlantic with appropriate respect.

Best season: September through March on North Atlantic groundswells. Water temperature in winter: 8–12°C (46–54°F) — a 5/4 wetsuit, hood, boots, and gloves are standard. Summer brings warmer water (16–18°C / 61–64°F) and larger crowds.

7. El Porto — Manhattan Beach, California

El Porto sits at the northern end of Manhattan Beach, just south of the Chevron oil refinery jetty, and is one of the most reliable beach breaks in Los Angeles. The wave receives both south swells in summer and northwest groundswells in winter, and the sand at El Porto tends to hold its shape better than many nearby Los Angeles County breaks, producing steep, punchy peaks that reward a more aggressive surfing style.

It is a city beach with all that implies — crowded on good weekends, easy to access, and operating within the full infrastructure of Los Angeles. But as a wave it punches above its weight: El Porto on a solid 1.5–2m (5–6ft) northwest groundswell with a light offshore can deliver hollow, powerful peaks that rival more celebrated California breaks.

Best season: October through March for northwest groundswells; June through August for south swells. Best in the early morning before the sea breeze arrives.

8. Salina Cruz — Oaxaca, Mexico

Salina Cruz is one of the great unexplored beach break destinations on the Pacific coast — a stretch of coast in southern Oaxaca, near the Isthmus of Tehuantepec, that receives consistent south swells and produces powerful, long-period beach break peaks across multiple beaches. The area is less developed and less visited than Puerto Escondido to the northwest, and the trade-off for the added difficulty in getting there is genuine crowds-to-quality ratio in the water.

The defining characteristic of Salina Cruz is consistency. The south swell machine runs through the region's main season with high frequency, and the flat stretches that occasionally interrupt Puerto Escondido are less common here. The town is a working port, not a resort, and the surf experience is correspondingly unglamorous — but for surfers who prioritise waves over amenities, it delivers.

Best season: May through September on south and southwest swells. The swell at Salina Cruz tends to be particularly powerful during the Mexican summer when trade winds in the South Pacific are most active.

9. Croyde Bay — Devon, UK

Croyde is the finest beach break in Devon — a compact, north-facing bay that funnels Atlantic groundswells into powerful, hollow peaks over a sand bottom. The wave is short and punchy rather than long and rolling, with steep take-offs and a fast, critical barrel section that rewards quick reflexes and a steep angle. It is consistently rated the best beach break in southwest England and produces the most impressive surfing of any beach in the region on a well-aimed northwest groundswell.

The village of Croyde is small and deliberately low-key — a few surf shops, pubs, and campsites surrounded by National Trust farmland. The wave attracts serious surfers from across the UK and Europe in autumn and winter, and the parking fields behind the beach fill up quickly on any morning with potential.

Best season: September through March on northwest Atlantic groundswells. Water temperature: similar to Fistral — 8–14°C (46–57°F) requiring full winter wetsuit equipment. Summer can produce smaller, quality surf on the right northwest swell but is generally less powerful.

10. Maroubra — Sydney, New South Wales, Australia

Maroubra is Sydney's most serious beach break — a south-facing bay that receives Southern Ocean swells more directly than most Sydney beaches and produces powerful, fast peaks with a reputation for consistency and, historically, intensity in the water. The local surfing identity at Maroubra — shaped by the Bra Boys and a working-class suburb with a strong community connection to the sea — is distinct from the more polished culture of beaches like Bondi and Manly.

The wave itself is genuinely good: steep, powerful, and capable of producing hollow barrels on the right swell and tide combination. It holds size better than nearby beaches and, on a solid southerly groundswell with a light northerly offshore, produces the kind of conditions that would be noteworthy on any coast.

Best season: March through September on southern ocean swells. Water temperature in winter: 17–19°C (63–66°F) — a 3/2 wetsuit is comfortable. Summer brings north and northeast swells with smaller, choppier conditions.

11. Lacanau — Gironde, France

Lacanau sits 45 kilometres north of Bordeaux on the Landes coast, north of Hossegor but sharing the same fundamental swell exposure: the full width of the North Atlantic, arriving as long-period groundswell on a straight, sandy coastline backed by pine forest. The beach is long, the sandbars shift constantly, and when a northwest groundswell arrives with an east wind, Lacanau produces powerful, hollow peaks that rival anything in the region.

The Lacanau Pro is one of the oldest and most storied surf competitions in Europe, and the town — purpose-built as a beach resort in the 1970s — has a lively summer surfing culture. As a destination it is slightly more resort-oriented than the raw, industrial Hossegor strip, which makes it easier to visit with family or non-surfers while still accessing genuinely good waves.

Best season: September through November for the best combination of consistent Atlantic swell and manageable conditions. The prime window extends October through March on larger groundswells.
